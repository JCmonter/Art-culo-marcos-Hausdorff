\subsection{Frames, nuclei, and open filters}
We begin by recalling some basic facts and terminology concerning frames, nuclei, and open filters. We refer to \cite{Johnstone82,PicadoPultr,simmonscollection,simmons2006assembly} for general background on frames and point-free topology.
A frame is a complete lattice \(A\) satisfying the infinite distributive law
\[a\wedge\bigvee X=\bigvee\{a\wedge x\mid x\in X\}\]
for every \(a\in A\) and \(X\subseteq A\). A frame homomorphism \(f\colon A\to B\) is a map preserving finite meets and arbitrary joins. Frames and frame homomorphisms form the category \(\Frm\).

As usual, the distributivity law determines a Heyting implication \(\succ\) on \(A\), characterized by
\[
x\wedge a\leq b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\leq(a\succ b).
\]
In particular, every frame homomorphism \(f\colon A\to B\), being a map preserving arbitrary joins, has a right adjoint \(f_*\colon B\to A\), characterized by
\[
f(a)\leq b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\leq f_*(b).
\]

A nucleus on a frame \(A\) is a map \(j\colon A\to A\) satisfying
\[
x\leq j(x),\qquad j(j(x))=j(x),\qquad
j(x\wedge y)=j(x)\wedge j(y)
\]
for all \(x,y\in A\). The set
\[
A_j=\{x\in A\mid (x)=x\}
\]
of fixed points of \(j\) is a frame, where arbitrary joins are given by
\[
\bigvee_{A_j}S=j\left(\bigvee_A S\right).
\]
Moreover, the map
\[
j^*\colon A\longrightarrow A_j,\qquad j^*(x)=j(x),
\]
is a surjective frame homomorphism. Conversely, every quotient of \(A\) arises in this way. We shall therefore identify nuclei on \(A\) with the corresponding quotients \(A_j\).
Three particular families of nuclei will be used throughout the paper. For each \(a\in A\), let
\[
u_a(x)=a\vee x,\qquad
v_a(x)=a\to x,\qquad
w_a(x)=(x\to a)\to a.
\]
The nuclei \(u_a\) and \(v_a\) determine, respectively, the closed and open quotient associated with \(a\), while the \(w_a\)'s will play an important role in the study of compact quotients below.

An element \(p\in A\) is \(\wedge\)-irreducible if \(p\neq 1\) and
\[
x\wedge y\leq p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x\leq p\ \text{ or }\ y\leq p.
\]
We denote by \(\pt(A)\) the set of all \(\wedge\)-irreducible elements of \(A\). These are in bijective correspondence with the completely prime filters of \(A\): to each \(p\in\pt(A)\) corresponds the completely prime filter
\[
F_p=\{a\in A\mid a\nleq p\}.
\]
Conversely, if \(P\) is a completely prime filter, then
\[
p_P=\bigvee(A\setminus P)
\]
is \(\wedge\)-irreducible and \(P=F_{p_P}\).

The point space of \(A\) is the set \(\pt(A)\) equipped with the topology generated by the sets
\[
\mathcal U(a)=\{p\in\pt(A)\mid a\nleq p\},
\qquad a\in A.
\]In fact, the assignment
\[
\mathcal U\colon A\longrightarrow\mathcal O(\pt(A)),
\qquad
a\longmapsto\mathcal U(a),
\]
is a surjective frame homomorphism.

The map \(\mathcal U\) is usually referred to as the spatial reflection of \(A\). A frame \(A\) is said to be spatial if \(\mathcal U\) is an isomorphism, that is, if
\[
A\cong\mathcal O(\pt(A)).
\]
Equivalently, \(A\) is spatial whenever its points separate its elements: for \(a,b\in A\),
\[
a\nleq b
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{there exists }p\in\pt(A)\text{ such that }b\leq p
\text{ and }a\nleq p.
\]

A filter \(F\) on a frame \(A\) is said to be open if, for every directed subset \(D\subseteq A\),
\[
\bigvee D\in F
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D\cap F\neq\varnothing.
\]
Thus, open filters are precisely those filters which are open with respect to the Scott topology on \(A\). We denote by \(A^\wedge\) the collection of all open filters on \(A\).

Open filters can be transferred along frame morphisms. Let
\[
f=f^*\colon A\longrightarrow B
\]
be a frame morphism with right adjoint \(f_*\colon B\to A\). If \(F\) and \(G\) are filters on \(A\) and \(B\), respectively, we define filters \(f^*F\) on \(B\) and \(f_*G\) on \(A\) by
\begin{equation}\label{Imagenfiltros}
b\in f^*F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f_*(b)\in F,
\qquad
a\in f_*G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(a)\in G.
\end{equation}
\begin{prop}\label{fF}
Let $f=f^*\colon A\to B$ be a frame morphism. If $G\in B^\wedge$, then $f_*G\in A^\wedge$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Clearly, \(f_*G\) is a filter on \(A\). Let \(X\subseteq A\) be directed and suppose that
\[
\bigvee X\in f_*G.
\]
Then \(f[X]\) is directed and, since \(f\) preserves arbitrary joins,
\[
\bigvee f[X]
=f\left(\bigvee X\right)\in G.
\]
Since \(G\) is open, there exists \(x\in X\) such that \(f(x)\in G\). Hence \(x\in f_*G\), and therefore \(f_*G\in A^\wedge\).
\end{proof}

Every nucleus \(j\) on \(A\) determines a filter
\[
\nabla(j)=\{a\in A\mid j(a)=1\},
\]
called the admissible filter of \(j\). We say that \(j\) admits \(a\in A\) whenever \(j(a)=1\).

Two nuclei \(j,k\) are said to be companions whenever
\[
\nabla(j)=\nabla(k).
\]
Each class of companion nuclei has a least element. These least elements are called fitted nuclei.

Given a filter \(F\) on \(A\), consider the pre-nucleus
\[
f_F=\bigvee_{a\in F}^{\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}}v_a,
\]
where the join is taken pointwise. Explicitly,
\[
f_F(x)=\bigvee_{a\in F}(a\succ x).
\]
Its transfinite iteration yields the least nucleus above \(f_F\), which we denote by
\[
v_F=f_F^\infty.
\]

Equivalently,
\[
v_F=\bigvee_{a\in F}v_a,
\]
where the join on the right-hand side is computed in the frame \(NA\) of nuclei on \(A\).

The nucleus \(v_F\) is the least nucleus on \(A\) admitting every element of \(F\). Consequently,
\[
F\subseteq\nabla(v_F).
\]
Moreover, if \(F\) is open, then
\[
\nabla(v_F)=F.
\]

Hence, the assignment
\[
F\longmapsto v_F
\]
gives a one-to-one correspondence between open filters and fitted nuclei whose admissible filters are open.

Open filters will play a central role throughout the paper. Besides determining fitted nuclei, they provide the point-free counterparts of compact saturated subsets through the Hofmann--Mislove correspondence, which we recall next.

Let \(S=\pt(A)\), endowed with its natural topology. We denote by \(\mathcal Q(S)\) the collection of compact saturated subsets of \(S\). The Hofmann--Mislove theorem establishes a bijective correspondence
\[
A^\wedge\longleftrightarrow\mathcal Q(\pt(A))
\]
between open filters on \(A\) and compact saturated subsets of its point space.

More explicitly, if \(F\in A^\wedge\), its corresponding compact saturated subset is
\[
Q_F=\{p\in\pt(A)\mid(\forall a\in F)\;a\nleq p\},
\]
whereas, for \(Q\in\mathcal Q(\pt(A))\), the corresponding open filter is
\[
F_Q=\{a\in A\mid(\forall p\in Q)\;a\nleq p\}.
\]
Thus,
\[
a\in F_Q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q\subseteq\mathcal U(a).
\]

When \(A=\mathcal O(S)\) for a sober space \(S\), this correspondence reduces to the classical Hofmann--Mislove theorem: the compact saturated subsets \(Q\subseteq S\) correspond bijectively to the open filters of \(\mathcal O(S)\) through
\[
Q\longmapsto\nabla(Q)
=\{U\in\mathcal O(S):Q\subseteq U\}.
\]
Whenever \(F\in A^\wedge\), we shall denote by \(Q\) its corresponding compact saturated subset of \(\pt(A)\), unless it is necessary to emphasize the dependence on \(F\). 
We shall refer to a pair \((F,Q)\), where \(F\in A^\wedge\) and \(Q\in\mathcal Q(\pt(A))\) correspond under the above bijection, as an HM pair.

\begin{obs}\label{Obs1}
Notice that the Hofmann--Mislove correspondence also includes the
degenerate case in which $A$ has no points. Indeed, if
$\pt(A)=\varnothing$, then the only compact saturated subset of
$\pt(A)$ is $\varnothing$, and hence
\[
    A^\wedge=\{A\}.
\]
Thus, the correspondence reduces to the single HM pair
\[
    (A,\varnothing).
\]
Since $0\in A$, the fitted nucleus associated with the improper open
filter $A$ is the top nucleus,
\[
    v_A=\top_{N(A)},
\]
and therefore induces the trivial quotient. Consequently, nontrivial
compact quotients arising from the Hofmann--Mislove correspondence can
only occur when $A$ admits proper open filters.
\end{obs}

The Hofmann--Mislove correspondence can be extended to include fitted nuclei. More precisely, the Hofmann--Mislove--Johnstone theorem establishes a correspondence 
between open filters, compact saturated subsets, and the associated fitted nuclei.

The preceding correspondence allows us to regard open filters as point-free representatives of compact saturated subsets. 
In the next subsection we shall see that they also determine compact quotients of \(A\), and we shall study the nuclei associated with such quotients.

\subsection{Compact Quotients and Admissibility Intervals}
We now turn our attention to compact quotients. Recall that every nucleus \(j\) on a frame \(A\) determines a quotient \(A_j\), and that its admissible filter is given by
\[
\nabla(j)=\{a\in A:j(a)=1\}.
\]
The following characterization of compact quotients is due to Sexton
\cite[Lemma 5.17]{Sexton2000}.
\begin{prop}
	Let \(j\) be a nucleus on a frame \(A\). Then the quotient \(A_j\) is compact if and only if \(\nabla(j)\) is an open filter.
\end{prop}

In particular, for every \(F\in A^\wedge\), the fitted nucleus \(v_F\) determines a compact quotient \(A_{v_F}\). Conversely, every compact quotient \(A_j\) determines the open filter \(\nabla(j)\in A^\wedge\).
Notice, however, that a compact quotient is not, in general, uniquely determined by its admissible filter. Thus, for a fixed \(F\in A^\wedge\), it is natural to consider all nuclei \(j\) satisfying
\[
\nabla(j)=F.
\]
As recalled above, \(v_F\) is the least such nucleus. We shall now describe the nuclei having \(F\) as their admissible filter and the corresponding interval of admissibility.

Let \(F\in A^\wedge\). Following the construction used in the general form of the H--M theorem, consider
\[
M_F=\{m\in A\setminus F\mid m\text{ is maximal}\}.
\]
Associated with \(F\), define
\[
w_F(x)=\bigwedge\{m\in M_F\mid x\leq m\},
\qquad x\in A,
\]
where the meet of the empty family is understood to be \(1\).

If \(\pt(A)\) is \(T_1\), then every point is maximal and, for the HM pair \((F,Q)\), one has
\[
M_F=Q.
\]

The nucleus \(w_F\) is the largest nucleus having \(F\) as its admissible filter. Hence
\[
\nabla(w_F)=F.
\]

It follows that the nuclei having \(F\) as their admissible filter form the interval
\[
[v_F,w_F]
=
\{j\in N(A)\mid v_F\leq j\leq w_F\}.
\]
We shall refer to \([v_F,w_F]\) as the admissibility interval associated with \(F\).

Evaluating the endpoints at \(0\), we obtain the interval
\[
[v_F(0),w_F(0)]\subseteq A,
\]
which will also play a central role in what follows.

We shall also need a particular class of nuclei arising from subspaces. Let \(S\) be a topological space, and denote by \(E^\circ\) the interior of a subset \(E\subseteq S\). For each \(E\subseteq S\), consider the map
\[
[E]\colon\mathcal O S\longrightarrow\mathcal O S,\qquad
[E](U)=(E\cup U)^\circ.
\]

It is straightforward to verify that \([E]\) is a nucleus on \(\mathcal O S\). 
A nucleus \(j\) on \(\mathcal O S\) is said to be spatially induced if \(j=[E]\) for some \(E\subseteq S\).

Spatially induced nuclei provide a natural representative within the admissibility interval associated with the neighbourhood filter of a compact saturated subset.
\begin{ej}
	Let \(Q\) be a compact saturated subset of a topological space \(S\), and consider its neighbourhood filter
\[
G=\nabla(Q)=\{U\in\mathcal O S\mid Q\subseteq U\}.
\]
By the Hofmann--Mislove theorem, \(G\in(\mathcal O S)^\wedge\). Consider the spatially induced nucleus \([Q']\), where \(Q'=S\setminus Q\). For every \(U\in\mathcal O S\),
\[
[Q'](U)=S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(Q'\cup U)^\circ=S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q'\cup U=S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q\subseteq U.
\]
Hence
\[
\nabla([Q'])=G.
\]
Since \(v_G\) and \(w_G\) are, respectively, the least and greatest nuclei having \(G\) as their admissible filter, it follows that
\[
v_G\leq[Q']\leq w_G.
\]
and evaluating in $\emptyset$, $v_G(\emptyset)\subseteq (Q')^\circ\subseteq w_G(\emptyset)$.
\end{ej}

More generally, let \(F\in A^\wedge\), let \(Q\subseteq\pt(A)\) be its corresponding compact saturated subset, and set \(G=\nabla(Q)\). 
We shall call \((F,Q,G)\) an HM triple.

\begin{prop}\label{morfismo} 
Let $(F,Q,G)$ be an HM triple. If \(k\in[v_G,w_G]\), then
\[
\mathcal{U}_*\circ k\circ \mathcal{U}\in[v_F,w_F].
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Since $k$ is a nucleus on $\mathcal{O}S$, the composite
\[
    \mathcal{U}_*\circ k\circ\mathcal{U}
\]
is a nucleus on $A$. Thus, it suffices to show that
\[
    \nabla(\mathcal{U}_*\circ k\circ\mathcal{U})=F.
\]
Indeed, for every $x\in A$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
x\in F
&\iff Q\subseteq\mathcal{U}(x)\\
&\iff \mathcal{U}(x)\in G\\
&\iff k(\mathcal{U}(x))=S\\
&\iff \mathcal{U}_*(k(\mathcal{U}(x)))=1\\
&\iff x\in\nabla(\mathcal{U}_*\circ k\circ\mathcal{U}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here, the third equivalence follows from $\nabla(k)=G$, while the
fourth follows from the adjunction $\mathcal{U}\dashv\mathcal{U}_*$
and $\mathcal{U}(1)=S$. Hence
\[
    \nabla(\mathcal{U}_*\circ k\circ\mathcal{U})=F,
\]
and therefore
\[
    \mathcal{U}_*\circ k\circ\mathcal{U}\in[v_F,w_F].
\]
\end{proof}

Proposition \ref{morfismo} defines a map
\[
\Phi\colon [v_G,w_G]\longrightarrow [v_F,w_F],
\qquad
\Phi(k)=\mathcal{U}_*\circ k\circ \mathcal{U}.
\]
In particular, for every $k\in[v_G,w_G]$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{comparacionvFwF}
v_F\leq \Phi(k) \leq w_F.
\end{equation}

We conclude this section by recording the behavior of spatially induced nuclei under this lifting in the spatial case.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:auxiliar}
Let \(A=\mathcal O S\) be spatial and let \(E\subseteq S\). Identifying \(S\) with \(\pt(\mathcal O S)\), we have
\[
\mathcal U_*\circ [E]\circ\mathcal U=[E].
\]
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(A=\mathcal O S\) is spatial,
\[
\mathcal U:\mathcal O S\longrightarrow\mathcal O(\pt(\mathcal O S))
\]
is an isomorphism. Let \(y\in\mathcal O S\). Since \(\mathcal U\) is surjective, there exists \(x\in\mathcal O S\) such that
\[
(E\cup\mathcal U(y))^\circ=\mathcal U(x).
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathcal U_*\circ[E]\circ\mathcal U)(y)
&=\mathcal U_*\bigl((E\cup\mathcal U(y))^\circ\bigr)\\
&=\mathcal U_*(\mathcal U(x))\\
&=x.
\end{aligned}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\mathcal U([E](y))
=(E\cup\mathcal U(y))^\circ
=\mathcal U(x).
\]
Since \(\mathcal U\) is injective, \([E](y)=x\). Therefore
\[
(\mathcal U_*\circ[E]\circ\mathcal U)(y)=[E](y)
\]
for every \(y\in\mathcal O S\), and consequently
\[
\mathcal U_*\circ[E]\circ\mathcal U=[E].
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Separation Properties Induced by Compact Quotients}\label{Definiciones}
The notions studied in this section are largely motivated by the work of Sexton and Simmons on point-sensitive and point-free patch constructions. 
In particular, Sexton introduced the notion of a neat frame in the study of the patch assembly. Although neatness was originally considered in that context, 
one of its consequences suggests a different point of view that will be central here: for every open filter \(F\), the fitted nucleus \(v_F\) determines a 
compact quotient which is also closed.

Our purpose is to study this phenomenon independently of its original role in patch constructions. More precisely, we are interested in identifying those 
nuclei associated with open filters that determine compact closed quotients, and in understanding what happens when this requirement is imposed not only on 
the fitted nuclei \(v_F\), but on other nuclei lying in their corresponding admissibility intervals. This perspective leads naturally to the notions introduced below.

\subsection{Neat frames}
We first recall Sexton's original definition of neatness (it is mentioned in \cite{sexton2006ordinal,sexton2006point, sexton2003}). Besides providing the starting point 
for the properties studied in this section, this formulation will make explicit the phenomenon concerning compact closed quotients that motivates our approach.
\begin{dfn}\label{Neat}
Let $A$ be a frame, let $F\in A^\wedge$ and $\alpha\in \Ord$. We say that:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $F$ is $\alpha$-neat, if 
	\[
	x\vee d=1, \quad \text{for every }x\in F,
	\]
	where $d=f^\alpha_F(0)$.
	\item $A$ is $\alpha$-neat if every $F\in A^\wedge$ is $\alpha$-neat.
	\item $A$ is neat if it is $\alpha$-neat for some $\alpha$. 
\end{itemize}
\end{dfn}

Let us point out a consequence of the preceding definition that will be relevant throughout this work. Suppose that \(A\) is \(\alpha\)-neat and let \(F\in A^\wedge\). 
If
\[
d=f_F^\alpha(0),
\]
then \(x\vee d=1\) for every \(x\in F\). Hence, \(u_d\) admits every element of \(F\), and therefore
\[
f_F\leq u_d.
\]
Since \(u_d\) is a nucleus, it follows that
\[
v_F=f_F^\infty\leq u_d.
\]
On the other hand, \(d=f_F^\alpha(0)\leq f_F^\infty(0)=v_F(0)\), and hence
\[
u_d\leq u_{v_F(0)}\leq v_F.
\]
Consequently,
\[
v_F=f_F^\infty=u_d.
\]
Thus, neatness ensures that the fitted nucleus associated with every open filter is a closed nucleus. Since \(v_F\) determines a compact quotient, every such quotient is 
simultaneously compact and closed.

Beyond this compact-closed quotient phenomenon, Sexton's study of neatness establishes connections with classical separation properties of topological spaces. 
This naturally raises the question of whether neatness and the related notions arising from her work admit analogous connections with point-free separation properties. 
Of particular relevance to our purposes is the spatial characterization of neatness in terms of packed and stacked spaces.

More precisely, Sexton proved that a topological space is neat if and only if it is both packed and stacked (\cite[Theorem 6.2]{sexton2006ordinal}). 

\subsection{Stacked and strongly stacked frames}
Motivated by the preceding spatial characterization, we now introduce point-free counterparts of the stacked and strongly stacked conditions.

We first recall the spatial notions introduced by Sexton. Let \(S\) be a topological space, let \(Q\subseteq S\) be compact saturated, and let
\[
G=\nabla(Q)\in(\mathcal O S)^\wedge
\]
be its corresponding open filter. Sexton calls \(S\) stacked if
\[
v_G(\emptyset)=(Q')^\circ,
\]
for every such pair \((G,Q)\), and strongly stacked if
\[
v_G=[Q']
\]
for every such pair.

We now return to the general setting. Let \(A\) be a frame, \(S=\pt(A)\), and let \((F,Q,G)\) be an H--M triple. Recall that the map of Proposition \eqref{morfismo}
\[
\Phi:[v_G,w_G]\longrightarrow[v_F,w_F],
\qquad
\Phi(k)=\mathcal U_*\circ k\circ\mathcal U,
\]
provides a natural way of comparing the admissibility interval associated with \(G\) with that associated with \(F\).
\begin{prop}\label{igualdadwF}
Let \((F,Q,G)\) be an H--M triple. Then \(\Phi(w_G)=w_F\), that is,
\[
w_F=\mathcal U_*\circ w_G\circ\mathcal U.
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition \ref{morfismo}, since $w_G\in[v_G,w_G]$, we have
\[
\mathcal{U}_*\circ w_G\circ \mathcal{U}\leq w_F.
\]
Thus, it remains to prove the reverse inequality.

By adjunction, it is enough to show that
\[
\mathcal{U}\circ w_F\leq w_G\circ \mathcal{U}.
\]
Let $x\in A$. Since $w_G=[M']$, we have
\[
w_G(\mathcal{U}(x))
=[M'](\mathcal{U}(x))
=(M'\cup \mathcal{U}(x))^\circ.
\]
Hence, we need to prove that
\[
\mathcal{U}(w_F(x))\subseteq (M'\cup \mathcal{U}(x))^\circ.
\]

Since $\mathcal{U}(w_F(x))$ is open, it is enough to show that
\[
\mathcal{U}(w_F(x))\subseteq M'\cup \mathcal{U}(x).
\]
Suppose, by contradiction, that there exists \(y\in \mathcal{U}(w_F(x))\) such that \(y\notin M'\)
and \(y\notin \mathcal{U}(x)\). Then $y\in M$ and $x\leq y$. On the other hand, \(y\in \mathcal{U}(w_F(x))\)
implies \(w_F(x)\nleq y\). However,
\[
w_F(x)=\bigwedge\{m\in M\mid x\leq m\},
\]
and since $y\in M$ with $x\leq y$, it follows that \(w_F(x)\leq y\) a contradiction.

Therefore, \(\mathcal{U}(w_F(x))\subseteq (M'\cup \mathcal{U}(x))^\circ\).
\end{proof}

The preceding proposition shows that \(\Phi\) preserves the upper endpoints of the corresponding admissibility intervals:
\[
\Phi(w_G)=w_F.
\]
It is therefore natural to ask to what extent the lower endpoints are also preserved. Motivated by Sexton's spatial notions, 
we consider two possible forms of this requirement: agreement at the bottom element, and agreement of the nuclei themselves.
Since \([Q']\) is the spatially induced nucleus appearing in Sexton's definitions, its image
\[
\Phi([Q'])=\mathcal U_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal U
\]
provides the natural candidate for reproducing this behavior on \(A\). This leads to the following definitions.

\begin{dfn}\label{def:apilado-marcos}
Let $A$ be a frame. We say that $A$ is \emph{stacked} if, for every HM pair $(F,Q)$,
    \[
    (U_*\circ[Q']\circ U)(0)=v_F(0).
    \]
\end{dfn}

\begin{dfn}\label{def:fapilado-marcos}
Let $A$ be a frame. We say that $A$ is \emph{strongly stacked} if, for every HM pair $(F,Q)$,
    \[
    U_*\circ[Q']\circ U=v_F;
    \]
\end{dfn}

Clearly, every strongly stacked frame is stacked.

\begin{obs}\label{Obs2}
If $\pt A=\varnothing$, then, by Remark \ref{Obs1}, the only HM pair is
$(A,\varnothing)$. Hence the strongly stacked condition is trivially
satisfied, and therefore so is the stacked condition. Thus, while both
notions are well defined for pointless frames, their nontrivial content
arises when $\pt A\neq\varnothing$.
\end{obs}

\subsection{KC frames}

Recall that neatness requires the fitted nucleus associated with each
open filter to determine a closed compact quotient. It is then natural
to ask what happens if this requirement is extended to every nucleus
admitting an open filter. This leads to a point-free counterpart of the
classical KC condition considered by Wilansky \cite{Wilansky1967}, where compact
subsets are required to be closed. Accordingly, we introduce the
following notion.

\begin{dfn}
Let $A$ be a frame. We say that $A$ is \emph{KC} if every compact quotient of $A$ is closed.
\end{dfn}

Equivalently, $A$ is KC if, for every $j\in NA$ such that
\[
\nabla(j)\in A^\wedge,
\]
there exists $a\in A$ such that
\[
j=u_a.
\]

In the spatial setting, Wilansky's KC condition coincides with
Rosemary's notion of packedness. Moreover, in this case one has
\[
    \text{Neat}
    \iff
    \text{Packed}
    =
    \text{KC and Stacked}.
\]
This naturally raises the question of whether analogous relationships
hold among the point-free notions introduced in this section. We shall
return to this question in Section \ref{Interaccion entre propiedades}. Before doing so, we first examine
how these notions interact with the classical separation axioms in
$\Frm$.

\section{Comparison with Classical Separation Axioms}\label{RelacionConPropiedadesDeSeparacion}

Using the conservativity of the stacked and strongly stacked
properties [Proposition \eqref{apiladoconservativa}], one can already observe that neither of these
properties is comparable with the $T_1$ separation axiom. Indeed,
there exist strongly stacked (and hence stacked) frames which are not
$T_1$, as well as $T_1$ frames which are not stacked (and hence not
strongly stacked).

\begin{ej}\label{T1nostacked}
\mbox{}
\begin{enumerate}	
\item The strongly stacked property does not imply $T_1$. Indeed, consider
the frame $A=[0,1]$ with its usual order. Its open filters are of the
form
\[
    F_r=(r,1], \qquad 0\leq r<1,
\]
and the corresponding compact saturated subsets of
$\pt A=[0,1)$ are
\[
    Q_r=[0,r].
\]
A direct computation shows that
\[
    v_{F_r}(x)=
    \begin{cases}
        x, & x\leq r,\\
        1, & x>r,
    \end{cases}
    =(\mathcal{U}_*\circ [Q_r']\circ \mathcal{U})(x).
\]
Hence, $A$ is strongly stacked. However, $A$ is not $T_1$, since
its points are not maximal.

\item The converse non-implications also hold. Indeed, the Hausdorff frame
of Paseka and Šmarda \cite{PasekaSmarda1992} considered in Example \eqref{HnoS} is
$T_1$, but it is not stacked. Consequently, it is not strongly
stacked either.
\end{enumerate}
\end{ej}


Recall that if a topological space $S$ is $T_1$ and strongly stacked, then
\[
w_G=v_G.
\]
where $G\in \mathcal{O}S^\wedge$. Moreover, every $T_1$ stacked space is sober. This naturally raises the
question of whether analogous results hold in the point-free setting. In this
section, we investigate this question.

\begin{prop}\label{maximosenpt}
Let $A$ be a frame. If
\[
v_F(0)=w_F(0)
\]
for every $F\in A^\wedge$, then every point of $A$ is maximal in $\pt A$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $p,q\in\pt A$ be such that $p\leq q$, and consider the open filter
\[
F_q=\{x\in A\mid x\nleq q\}.
\]
Since $A\setminus F_q=\downarrow q$, it follows directly from the
definition of $w_{F_q}$ that
\begin{equation}\label{igualdadwF_p}
w_{F_q}=w_q.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
w_{F_q}(0)=q.
\]

By hypothesis,
\[
q=w_{F_q}(0)=v_{F_q}(0)
=\bigvee_{x\in F_q}\neg x.
\]
For every $x\in F_q$, we have $x\nleq q$. Since $p\leq q$, it follows
that $x\nleq p$. Moreover,
\[
x\wedge\neg x=0\leq p.
\]
Since $p$ is a point, it is meet-irreducible, and therefore
\[
\neg x\leq p.
\]
Thus,
\[
q=v_{F_q}(0)
=\bigvee_{x\in F_q}\neg x
\leq p.
\]
Since $p\leq q$, we conclude that $p=q$. Hence, every point of $A$ is
maximal in 
$\pt A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{focales}
Let $A$ be a frame. The following statements hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $A$ is $T_1$ and stacked, then
    \[
    v_F(0)=w_F(0)
    \]
    for every $F\in A^\wedge$.

    \item If
    \[
    v_F(0)=w_F(0)
    \]
    for every $F\in A^\wedge$, then $A$ is stacked.

    \item Suppose that
    \[
    v_F(0)=w_F(0)
    \]
    for every $F\in A^\wedge$ and that
    \[
    \mathcal{V}(a)=\{q\in\pt A\mid a\leq q\}\neq\varnothing
    \]
    for every $a\neq 1\in A$. Then $A$ is $T_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
	\mbox{}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Let $(F,Q)$ be an HM pair. Since $A$ is $T_1$, the space
$S=\pt A$ is $T_1$. Hence $Q=M$. Since $A$ is stacked,
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_F(0)
&=(\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U})(0)\\
&=(\mathcal{U}_*\circ[M']\circ\mathcal{U})(0)\\
&=w_F(0).
\end{aligned}
\]

\item Let $(F,Q)$ be an HM pair. Recall that
\begin{equation}\label{interad}
v_F
\leq
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}
\leq
w_F.
\end{equation}
Evaluating at $0$, we obtain
\[
v_F(0)
\leq
(\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U})(0)
\leq
w_F(0).
\]
By hypothesis, $v_F(0)=w_F(0)$, and hence
\[
v_F(0)
=
(\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U})(0).
\]
Therefore, $A$ is stacked.

\item Let $p\in\pt A$ and $a\in A$ be such that
\[
p\leq a.
\]
By hypothesis, there exists $q\in\pt A$ such that
\[
a\leq q.
\]
Hence,
\[
p\leq a\leq q.
\]
By Proposition \ref{maximosenpt}, every point of $A$ is maximal. Therefore, $p=q$, and
consequently
\[
p\leq a\leq p.
\]
Thus, $a=p$, and hence $A$ is $T_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

Thus, in the $T_1$ case, the stacked condition forces the lower and upper
bounds of every admissibility interval to agree at $0$. The strongly stacked
case yields the corresponding equality of the nuclei themselves.

\begin{cor}\label{vF=wF}
Let $A$ be a frame. The following statements hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $A$ is $T_1$ and strongly stacked, then
    \[
    v_F=w_F
    \]
    for every $F\in A^\wedge$.

    \item If
    \[
    v_F=w_F
    \]
    for every $F\in A^\wedge$, then $A$ is strongly stacked.

    \item Suppose that
    \[
    v_F=w_F
    \]
    for every $F\in A^\wedge$ and that
    \[
    \mathcal{V}(a)=\{q\in\pt A\mid a\leq q\}\neq\varnothing
    \]
    for every $a\in A$. Then $A$ is $T_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
\mbox{}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Let $(F,Q)$ be an HM pair. Since $A$ is strongly stacked,
\[
v_F=\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Moreover, since $A$ is $T_1$, we have $Q=M$. Hence, by Proposition \ref{igualdadwF},
\[
v_F
=\mathcal{U}_*\circ[M']\circ\mathcal{U}
=w_F.
\]

\item By \eqref{interad},
\[
v_F
\leq
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}
\leq
w_F.
\]
Thus, if $v_F=w_F$, then
\[
v_F=\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U},
\]
and therefore $A$ is strongly stacked.

\item Since $v_F=w_F$ implies
\[
v_F(0)=w_F(0),
\]
the result follows from Proposition \ref{focales} (3).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

While the strongly stacked condition yields the collapse of every
admissibility interval, the stacked condition already guarantees this
phenomenon for those associated with completely prime filters. This gives the
following consequence.

\begin{cor}\label{Igualdadnucleos}
Let $A$ be a $T_1$ stacked frame. Then every admissibility interval
associated with a completely prime filter collapses to a singleton. More
precisely, for every $p\in\pt A$,
\[
[v_{F_p},w_{F_p}]=\{u_p\}.
\]
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
Let $p\in\pt A$. Since $A$ is $T_1$ and stacked, Proposition \ref{focales} gives
\[
v_{F_p}(0)=w_{F_p}(0)=p.
\]
Moreover, as $p$ is maximal,
\[
w_{F_p}=w_p=u_p.
\]

Recall that, for every nucleus $j$, one has
\[
u_{j(0)}\leq j.
\]
Indeed, for every $x\in A$,
\[
j(0)\vee x\leq j(x).
\]
Applying this to $j=v_{F_p}$ yields
\[
u_p=u_{v_{F_p}(0)}\leq v_{F_p}.
\]
On the other hand, by definition of the admissibility interval,
\[
v_{F_p}\leq w_{F_p}=u_p.
\]
Therefore,
\[
[v_{F_p},w_{F_p}]=\{u_p\}.
\]
\end{proof}

is not injective.

There exist non-spatial $T_1$ strongly stacked frames. In particular, there

The previous example shows that the $T_1$ and stacked conditions do not imply
spatiality. We now compare stackedness with the stronger separation property
$\mathbf{(H)}$. Recall that a frame $A$ satisfies property $\mathbf{(H)}$ if
\[
    1\neq a\nleq b
\]
implies that there exists $c\in A$ such that
\[
    c\nleq a
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \neg c\nleq b.
\]
As shown in \cite{PasekaSmarda1992}, property $\mathbf{(H)}$ implies $T_1$. Nevertheless, property
$\mathbf{(H)}$ does not imply stackedness, as the following example shows.

\begin{ej}\label{HnoS}
There exists a frame satisfying property $\mathbf{(H)}$ which is not stacked.

Let $I=[0,1]$, endowed with its usual topology, and consider the frame
\[
    A=\mathcal{O}(I).
\]
Since $I$ is a compact Hausdorff space, $A$ is a compact Hausdorff frame.
Moreover, since $I$ is $T_1$, every point of $A$ is maximal and
\[
    D(A)=\pt A
    =
    \{I\setminus\{x\}\mid x\in I\},
\]
where $D(A)$ denotes the set of maximal elements of $A$.

Following the construction of Paseka and \v{S}marda
\cite[Proposition 2.3]{PasekaSmarda1992}, let
\[
    \widetilde{A}
    =
    \{(u,v)\in A\times A_{\neg\neg}\mid u\leq v\},
\]
where
\[
    A_{\neg\neg}=\{a\in A\mid w_0(a)=a\}
\]
is the Boolean algebra of regular elements of $A$. By
\cite[Propositions 2.3 and 2.4]{PasekaSmarda1992}, $\widetilde{A}$ is a compact frame. 
In particular, $\widetilde{A}$ satisfies property $\mathbf{(H)}$.

The maximal elements of $\widetilde{A}$ are precisely the elements of the
form
\[
    D(\widetilde{A})=\{(d,1)\mid d\in D(A)\}.
\]

Since $\widetilde{A}$ satisfies $\mathbf{(H)}$, it is $T_1$, and hence its points
are precisely its maximal elements. Therefore,
\[
    \pt\widetilde{A}
    =
    \{(I\setminus\{x\},I):x\in I\}.
\]

Since $\widetilde{A}$ is compact,
\[
    F=\uparrow 1=\{1\}
\]
is an open filter. Let $(F,Q)$ be the corresponding HM pair. By the
Hofmann--Mislove correspondence,
\[
    Q=\pt\widetilde{A}-F.
\]
Since $1\notin\pt\widetilde{A}$, it follows that
\[
    Q=\pt\widetilde{A}.
\]

Now, since $F=\{1\}$,
\[
    f_F=v_1=\id_{\widetilde{A}},
\]
and therefore
\[
    v_F(0)=0_{\widetilde{A}}.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    (\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ \mathcal{U})(0)
    &=
    \bigwedge Q\\
    &=
    \bigwedge\pt\widetilde{A}\\
    &=
    (\varnothing,I)
    \neq
    (\varnothing,\varnothing)
    =
    v_F(0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus,
\[
    v_F(0)\neq(\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ \mathcal{U})(0),
\]
and consequently $\widetilde{A}$ is not stacked.
\end{ej}

Consequently, although property $\mathbf{(H)}$ implies $T_1$, it does not imply
stackedness. Indeed, the preceding example provides a compact Hausdorff
frame which is not stacked.

Together with the strongly stacked non-spatial example above, this
further illustrates the distinction between separation properties and
stackedness in the point-free setting. On the one hand, there exist
non-spatial $T_1$ strongly stacked frames; on the other hand, even the
stronger Hausdorff condition $\mathbf{(H)}$ does not guarantee stackedness.

At this point, it is useful to consider another point-free version of
the Hausdorff separation axiom. A frame $A$ is said to be
strongly Hausdorff, or simply $\mathbf{(sH)}$, if the codiagonal
\[
    \nabla_A:A\oplus A\longrightarrow A
\]
is a closed quotient. Strong Hausdorffness retains the expected
Hausdorff behavior on spatial instances, but, unlike $\mathbf{(H)}$, it is not
a conservative extension of the classical $T_2$ axiom. Nevertheless,
this stronger condition has an important consequence for the compact
quotients considered here.

\begin{prop}\label{sHimplicaKC}
Every strongly Hausdorff frame is KC.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
This follows directly from of
\cite[Theorem~9.2.1]{picado2021separation}, which states that every compact quotient of a
strongly Hausdorff frame is closed.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{KCT1}
Every KC frame is $T_1$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $p\in\pt A$. The filter
\[
F_p=\nabla(w_p)=\{x\in A\mid x\nleq p\}
\]
is completely prime and, in particular, $F_p\in A^\wedge$. Hence $A_{w_p}$
is a compact quotient of $A$. Since $A$ is KC, this quotient is closed.
Therefore,
\[
w_p=u_a
\]
for some $a\in A$. Evaluating at $0$, we obtain
\[
a=u_a(0)=w_p(0)=p,
\]
and hence
\[
w_p=u_p.
\]

Now let $a\in A$ be such that
\[
p<a\leq1.
\]
Since $a\nleq p$, we have
\[
w_p(a)=1.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
u_p(a)=p\vee a=a.
\]
Since $w_p=u_p$, it follows that
\[
a=1.
\]
Thus $p$ is maximal. Since every point of $A$ is maximal, $A$ is $T_1$.
\end{proof}

We briefly recall two separation properties that will be useful in what
follows. A frame \(A\) is said to be fit if, whenever
\(a\not\leq b\), there exist \(x,y\in A\) such that
\[
a\vee x=1,\qquad y\not\leq b,\qquad x\wedge y\leq b.
\]
On the other hand, \(A\) is said to be subfit if, whenever
\(a\not\leq b\), there exists \(c\in A\) such that
\[
a\vee c=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b\vee c\neq 1.
\]
Every fit frame is subfit. We will denote these properties by
\(\mathbf{(fit)}\) and \(\mathbf{(sfit)}\), respectively.

\begin{cor}
Every subfit KC frame is spatial.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
Let $A$ be a subfit KC frame. Since every KC frame is $T_1$, the
result follows from \cite[Proposition~2.5.3]{picado2021separation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}\label{KCnosfit}
There exists a KC frame which is strongly stacked and not subfit.
Consequently,
\[
    \mathrm{KC}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{(sfit)}.
\]
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Consider the frame
\[
    A=B\times 2,
\]
introduced in Example \ref{T1+S no espacial}. Recall that $A$ is $T_1$, strongly stacked,
and non-spatial.

The only open filters of $A$ are $A$ and $F_p=\uparrow(0,1)$, where $p=(1,0)$ is the unique point of $A$.

For $F=A$, we have
\[
    v_A=w_A=\operatorname{tp}=u_1.
\]

On the other hand, for $F=F_p$, by Corollary \ref{Igualdadnucleos} yield
\[
    v_{F_p}=w_{F_p}=u_p.
\]

Thus, for every $F\in A^\wedge$, every nucleus in the admissibility
interval $[v_F,w_F]$ is closed. Hence $A$ is a KC frame.

Finally, $A$ cannot be subfit. Indeed, every $T_1$ subfit frame is
spatial, whereas $A$ is $T_1$ and non-spatial. Consequently, $A$ is
a KC frame which is strongly stacked and not subfit. In particular,
\[
    \mathrm{KC}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{(sfit)}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Inheritance and preservation properties}\label{Permanencia}

The notions of stacked and strongly stacked frames introduced above are
formulated in terms of the admissibility intervals associated with
Hofmann--Mislove pairs. In particular, they describe how the lower
endpoint determined by a fitted nucleus compares with its spatial
counterpart. A closely related phenomenon had already appeared in the
work of Simmons.

More precisely, Simmons shows in \cite[Lemma 8.9 and Corollary 8.10]{simmons2004vietoris}  that, for a Hofmann--Mislove pair
\((F,Q)\), with \(G=\nabla(Q)\), the diagram
\begin{equation}\label{Qlaxo}
\begin{tikzcd}
    A \arrow[r,"v_F"] \arrow[d,"\mathcal{U}"'] &
    A \arrow[d,"\mathcal{U}"] \\
    \mathcal{O}S \arrow[r,"v_G"'] &
    \mathcal{O}S
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
commutes laxly; that is,
\[
    \mathcal{U}\circ v_F\leq v_G\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Here
\[
    v_F=f_F^\infty
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    v_G=f_G^\infty
\]
are the fitted nuclei associated with \(F\in A^\wedge\) and
\(G\in(\mathcal{O}S)^\wedge\), respectively.

In the terminology developed in the preceding sections, Simmons'
inequality may be viewed as an early manifestation of the behavior
underlying our notions of stackedness: it provides a canonical
comparison between the lower endpoints of the corresponding
admissibility intervals in \(A\) and \(\mathcal{O}S\).

We now show that this phenomenon is not restricted to the spatial
reflection. In fact, an analogous comparison holds for an arbitrary
frame morphism. Let
\[
    h\colon A\longrightarrow B
\]
be a frame morphism, let \(G\in B^\wedge\), and set
\[
    F=h_*G\in A^\wedge.
\]
We shall compare the fitted nuclei determined by these filters through
the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
    A \arrow[r,"v_F"] \arrow[d,"h"'] &
    A \arrow[d,"h"] \\
    B \arrow[r,"v_G"'] &
    B .
\end{tikzcd}
\]

As in Simmons' situation, the relevant comparison is first established
at the level of the prenuclei whose transfinite iterations generate the
corresponding fitted nuclei. The following lemma provides the basic
inequality needed for this purpose.

\begin{lem}\label{f1f}
Let \(h\colon A\to B\) be a frame morphism, \(G\in B^\wedge\), and
\(F=h_*G\). Then
\[
    h\circ f_F\leq f_G\circ h.
\]
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
Recall that, by \eqref{Imagenfiltros},
\[
    F=h_*G=\{a\in A\mid h(a)\in G\}.
\]
Hence
\[
    f_F=\bigvee\{v_a\mid a\in F\}
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    f_G=\bigvee\{v_y\mid y\in G\}.
\]

Let \(x\in A\). For every \(a\in F\), we have \(h(a)\in G\), and
therefore
\[
    h(v_a(x))
    =h(a\to x)
    \leq h(a)\to h(x)
    =v_{h(a)}(h(x))
    \leq f_G(h(x)).
\]
Thus, since \(h\) preserves arbitrary joins,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    h(f_F(x))
    &=
    h\left(\bigvee_{a\in F}v_a(x)\right)\\
    &=
    \bigvee_{a\in F}h(v_a(x))\\
    &\leq f_G(h(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
    h\circ f_F\leq f_G\circ h.
\]
\end{proof}

The preceding inequality is preserved throughout the transfinite
iteration of the corresponding prenuclei.

\begin{lem}\label{finftyf}
Let \(h\colon A\to B\) be a frame morphism, \(G\in B^\wedge\), and
\(F=h_*G\). Then, for every ordinal \(\alpha\),
\[
    h\circ f_F^\alpha\leq f_G^\alpha\circ h.
\]
In particular,
\[
    h\circ v_F\leq v_G\circ h.
\]
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
We proceed by transfinite induction on \(\alpha\). If \(\alpha=0\),
the result is immediate.

For the successor step, suppose that the inequality holds for
\(\alpha\). Then, by Lemma \ref{f1f} and the induction hypothesis,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    h\circ f_F^{\alpha+1}
    &=h\circ f_F\circ f_F^\alpha\\
    &\leq f_G\circ h\circ f_F^\alpha\\
    &\leq f_G\circ f_G^\alpha\circ h\\
    &=f_G^{\alpha+1}\circ h.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally, let \(\lambda\) be a limit ordinal. For every \(a\in A\),
since \(h\) preserves arbitrary joins, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
    h\bigl(f_F^\lambda(a)\bigr)
    &=
    h\left(\bigvee_{\alpha<\lambda}f_F^\alpha(a)\right)\\
    &=
    \bigvee_{\alpha<\lambda}h\bigl(f_F^\alpha(a)\bigr)\\
    &\leq
    \bigvee_{\alpha<\lambda}f_G^\alpha(h(a))\\
    &=
    f_G^\lambda(h(a)),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the inequality follows from the induction hypothesis. Hence
\[
    h\circ f_F^\lambda\leq f_G^\lambda\circ h.
\]

Since
\[
    v_F=f_F^\infty
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    v_G=f_G^\infty,
\]
taking an ordinal at which both transfinite iterations have stabilized
yields
\[
    h\circ v_F\leq v_G\circ h.
\]
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{tidyquout}
If \(A\) is a neat frame, then \(A_j\) is a neat frame.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that \(A\) is \(\alpha\)-neat. Let \(G\in A_j^\wedge\) and set
\[
    F=j_*G\in A^\wedge.
\]
Consider the corresponding prenuclei
\[
    f_F=\bigvee_{x\in F}v_x
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    f_G=\bigvee_{y\in G}v_y.
\]

Since \(A\) is \(\alpha\)-neat, for every \(x\in F\),
\[
    x\vee\widehat d=1,
    \qquad
    \text{where }
    \widehat d=f_F^\alpha(0).
\]
Set
\[
    d=f_G^\alpha(0).
\]

By Proposition \ref{finftyf}, applied to the frame morphism
\(j=j^*\colon A\to A_j\),
\[
    j(\widehat d)
    =j\bigl(f_F^\alpha(0)\bigr)
    \leq f_G^\alpha(j(0))
    =f_G^\alpha(0)
    =d.
\]

Since \(j\) is inflationary,
\[
    \widehat d\leq j(\widehat d)\leq d.
\]

Now let \(y\in G\). Since \(y\in A_j\), we have \(j(y)=y\).
Thus, by \eqref{Imagenfiltros},
\[
    y\in F=j_*G.
\]
Consequently,
\[
    y\vee\widehat d=1.
\]
Since \(\widehat d\leq d\), it follows that
\[
    1
    =y\vee\widehat d
    \leq y\vee d
    \leq1.
\]
Hence
\[
    y\vee d=1.
\]
Therefore, \(A_j\) is \(\alpha\)-neat, and hence neat.
\end{proof}

The following proposition establishes that neatness is preserved under
binary coproducts.

\begin{prop}\label{tidyplus}
Let \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) be \(\alpha\)-neat frames. Then
\(A_1\oplus A_2\) is \(\alpha\)-neat.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
    \iota_i\colon A_i\longrightarrow A_1\oplus A_2,
    \qquad i=1,2,
\]
be the canonical frame morphisms, and let
\[
    F\in (A_1\oplus A_2)^\wedge.
\]
For \(i=1,2\), set
\[
    F_i=(\iota_i)_*F\in A_i^\wedge.
\]
Since \(A_i\) is \(\alpha\)-neat, if
\[
    d_i=f_{F_i}^{\alpha}(0),
\]
then
\[
    x_i\vee d_i=1
\]
for every \(x_i\in F_i\).

By the definition of \(F_i=(\iota_i)_*F\),
\[
    x_i\in F_i
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \iota_i(x_i)\in F.
\]
Under the usual representation of the binary coproduct, this gives
\[
    x_1\oplus 1,\;1\oplus x_2\in F.
\]
Since \(F\) is a filter,
\[
    (x_1\oplus 1)\wedge(1\oplus x_2)
    =x_1\oplus x_2\in F.
\]

Now set
\[
    d=f_F^\alpha(0\oplus0).
\]
By Proposition \ref{finftyf}, applied to the canonical morphisms
\(\iota_i\), we have
\[
    \iota_i\circ f_{F_i}^\alpha
    \leq
    f_F^\alpha\circ\iota_i,
    \qquad i=1,2.
\]
Evaluating at \(0\in A_i\), we obtain
\[
    \iota_1(d_1)
    \leq f_F^\alpha(0\oplus1)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \iota_2(d_2)
    \leq f_F^\alpha(1\oplus0).
\]
Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    d_1\oplus d_2
    &=(d_1\oplus1)\wedge(1\oplus d_2)\\
    &=\iota_1(d_1)\wedge\iota_2(d_2)\\
    &\leq
    f_F^\alpha(0\oplus1)
    \wedge
    f_F^\alpha(1\oplus0)\\
    &=f_F^\alpha\bigl((0\oplus1)\wedge(1\oplus0)\bigr)\\
    &=f_F^\alpha(0\oplus0)\\
    &=d.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally, for \(x_i\in F_i\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
    1\oplus1
    &=(x_1\vee d_1)\oplus(x_2\vee d_2)\\
    &=(x_1\oplus x_2)\vee(d_1\oplus d_2)\\
    &\leq(x_1\oplus x_2)\vee d\\
    &\leq1\oplus1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
    (x_1\oplus x_2)\vee d=1\oplus1.
\]
Thus \(A_1\oplus A_2\) is \(\alpha\)-neat.
\end{proof}

\begin{cor}\label{Paso 3}
Let $A_1$ and $A_2$ be $\alpha$-neat and $\beta$-neat, respectively. Then $A_1\oplus A_2$ is $\gamma$-neat, where
\[
\gamma=\max\{\alpha, \beta\}.
\]
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
Let $\gamma=\max\{\alpha, \beta\}$. Then $A_1$ and $A_2$ are both $\gamma$-neat. Hence, by Proposition \ref{tidyplus}, $A_1\oplus A_2$ is $\gamma$-neat.
\end{proof}

We extend the previous result to arbitrary coproducts.

\begin{thm}\label{coprodE}
Neatness is preserved under arbitrary coproducts.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\{A_i\}_{i\in I}\) be a family of neat frames, and for each
\(i\in I\), let \(\alpha_i\) be a neatness degree of \(A_i\). Consider
\[
    A=\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_i
\]
and let
\[
    \iota_i\colon A_i\longrightarrow A
\]
denote the canonical coproduct morphisms.

Let \(F\in A^\wedge\). For each \(i\in I\), set
\[
    F_i=(\iota_i)_*F\in A_i^\wedge.
\]
Moreover,
\[
    x_i\in F_i
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \iota_i(x_i)\in F.
\]

Set
\[
    \alpha=\sup\{\alpha_i\mid i\in I\}.
\]
Then every \(A_i\) is \(\alpha\)-neat. Hence, for every
\(x_i\in F_i\),
\[
    x_i\vee d_i=1,
\]
where
\[
    d_i=f_{F_i}^{\alpha}(0).
\]

Let
\[
    f_F=\bigvee_{x\in F}v_x
\]
be the prenucleus associated with \(F\). By Proposition \ref{finftyf}, applied
to each canonical coproduct morphism
\(\iota_i\colon A_i\to A\), we have
\[
    \iota_i\circ f_{F_i}^{\alpha}
    \leq
    f_F^{\alpha}\circ\iota_i.
\]
Evaluating at \(0\in A_i\), we obtain
\[
    \iota_i(d_i)
    =
    \iota_i\bigl(f_{F_i}^{\alpha}(0)\bigr)
    \leq
    f_F^{\alpha}(\iota_i(0))
    =
    f_F^{\alpha}(\langle0\rangle).
\]

Set
\[
    d=f_F^{\alpha}(\langle0\rangle).
\]
Therefore,
\[
    \iota_i(d_i)\leq d
\]
for every \(i\in I\). Taking arbitrary joins yields
\[
    \langle d_i\rangle
    \vee
    \bigvee_{i\in I}\iota_i(d_i)
    \leq d.
\]

Now let
\[
    \langle x_i\rangle\in F.
\]
Since \(x_i\vee d_i=1\) for every \(i\in I\), we have
\[
    \langle x_i\rangle\vee\langle d_i\rangle
    =
    \langle x_i\vee d_i\rangle
    =
    \langle1\rangle.
\]
As \(\langle d_i\rangle\leq d\),
\[
    \langle1\rangle
    =
    \langle x_i\rangle\vee\langle d_i\rangle
    \leq
    \langle x_i\rangle\vee d
    \leq
    \langle1\rangle.
\]
Thus,
\[
    \langle x_i\rangle\vee d=\langle1\rangle.
\]
Therefore,
\[
    A=\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_i
\]
is \(\alpha\)-neat, and hence neat.
\end{proof} 

The preceding results have a categorical consequence. Indeed, the
subcategory of neat frames is closed under quotients, by
Proposition \ref{tidyquout}, and under arbitrary coproducts, by
Proposition \ref{coprodE}. Hence, the standard criterion for coreflectivity of
full subcategories of \(\Frm\) applies.
\begin{cor}
The subcategory of neat frames is a coreflective subcategory of $\Frm$.
\end{cor}

We now turn to the inheritance of the stronger properties introduced
above. We first show that strong stackedness is inherited by quotients,
and then establish the analogous result for the KC property.

Recall that, for a frame morphism $f\colon A\to B$, $j\in NA$, and
$k\in NB$, we have
\[
N(f)(j)
=
\bigvee\{u_{f(j(a))}\wedge v_{f(a)}\mid a\in A\}
\in NB
\]
and
\[
N(f)_*(k)=f_*\circ k\circ f\in NA,
\]
where $f_*$ denotes the right adjoint of $f$.

\begin{prop}\label{Cocientefapilado}
Let $A$ be a strongly stacked frame and $j\in NA$. Then $A_j$ is strongly
stacked.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $F\in A_j^\wedge$, and set
\[
S=\pt A,
\qquad
S_j=\pt A_j.
\]
Consider the open filter
\[
G=j_*(F)=\{x\in A\mid j(x)\in F\},
\]
and let
\[
Q=S_j\setminus F,
\qquad
R=S\setminus G.
\]
Denote by
\[
\mathcal{U}\colon A\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}S
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathcal{U}_j\colon A_j\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}S_j
\]
the corresponding spatial reflections.

Set
\[
l=N(\mathcal{U}_j)_*([Q'])
=(\mathcal{U}_j)_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}_j
\]
and
\[
m=N(j^*)(v_G).
\]
By Proposition \ref{morfismo},
\[
v_F\leq l.
\]
Thus, it is enough to prove
\[
l\leq m\leq v_F.
\]

We first prove that $m\leq v_F$. Since $G=j_*(F)$, the fitted nuclei
associated with $G$ and $F$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq1}
j^*\circ v_G\leq v_F\circ j^*.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
m=N(j^*)(v_G)
=
\bigvee_{x\in A}
\left(
u_{j^*(v_G(x))}\wedge v_{j^*(x)}
\right).
\]
By \eqref{eq1}, for every $x\in A$,
\[
j^*(v_G(x))\leq v_F(j^*(x)),
\]
and hence
\[
u_{j^*(v_G(x))}
\leq
u_{v_F(j^*(x))}.
\]
Therefore,
\[
u_{j^*(v_G(x))}\wedge v_{j^*(x)}
\leq
u_{v_F(j^*(x))}\wedge v_{j^*(x)}
\leq v_F.
\]
Taking joins over $x\in A$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq2}
m\leq v_F.
\end{equation}

We now prove that $l\leq m$. Since $A$ is strongly stacked,
\[
v_G=N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])
=\mathcal{U}_*\circ[R']\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Hence,
\[
m
=
N(j^*)\bigl(N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])\bigr).
\]

To compare the $u$-components in the decompositions of $l$ and $m$, consider
the auxiliary nucleus
\[
n=N(\mathcal{U}_j\circ j^*)_*([Q']).
\]
We first observe that, for every $x\in A_j$,
\[
j^*(n(x))=l(x).
\]
Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
j^*(n(x))
&=
j^*\left(
j_*\circ(\mathcal{U}_j)_*
\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}_j\circ j^*
\right)(x)\\
&=
j^*j_*\left(
\bigvee
\left\{
a\in A_j
\;\middle|\;
\mathcal{U}_j(a)
\subseteq
Q'\cup\mathcal{U}_j(j^*(x))
\right\}
\right)\\
&=
\bigvee
\left\{
a\in A_j
\;\middle|\;
\mathcal{U}_j(a)
\subseteq
Q'\cup\mathcal{U}_j(x)
\right\}\\
&=l(x).
\end{aligned}
\]

Now, under the usual identification of $S_j$ with the corresponding subspace
of $S$, we have
\[
Q=R\cap S_j.
\]
Using the decomposition of a nucleus in terms of its $u$- and $v$-components,
we have
\[
l
=
\bigvee_{x\in A_j}
\left(
u_{l(x)}\wedge v_x
\right)
=
\bigvee_{x\in A_j}
\left(
u_{j^*(n(x))}\wedge v_x
\right).
\]

On the other hand, since
\[
m
=
N(j^*)\bigl(N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])\bigr),
\]
we have
\[
m
=
\bigvee_{a\in A}
\left(
u_{j^*(N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])(a))}
\wedge
v_{j^*(a)}
\right).
\]

For $x\in A_j$, the explicit description of the right adjoint gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
j^*\bigl(N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])(x)\bigr)
&=
j^*\left(
\bigvee
\left\{
a\in A
\;\middle|\;
\mathcal{U}(a)
\subseteq
R'\cup\mathcal{U}(x)
\right\}
\right)\\
&=
\bigvee
\left\{
j^*(a)\in A_j
\;\middle|\;
\mathcal{U}(j^*(a))
\subseteq
Q'\cup\mathcal{U}(x)
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, since $x\in A_j$,
\[
\mathcal{U}(j^*(a))
\subseteq
Q'\cup\mathcal{U}(x)
\]
is equivalent, after restriction to $S_j$, to
\[
\mathcal{U}_j(j^*(a))
\subseteq
Q'\cup\mathcal{U}_j(x).
\]
Consequently,
\[
j^*(n(x))
\leq
j^*\bigl(N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])(x)\bigr).
\]
Therefore,
\[
u_{j^*(n(x))}
\leq
u_{j^*(N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])(x))}
\]
for every $x\in A_j$, and hence
\[
u_{l(x)}\wedge v_x
=
u_{j^*(n(x))}\wedge v_x
\leq
u_{j^*(N(\mathcal{U})_*([R'])(x))}\wedge v_x
\leq m.
\]
Taking joins over $x\in A_j$, we obtain
\[
l\leq m.
\]

Combining this inequality with \eqref{eq2} and $v_F\leq l$, we obtain
\[
v_F\leq l\leq m\leq v_F.
\]
Hence,
\[
v_F=l
=
(\mathcal{U}_j)_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}_j.
\]
Therefore, $A_j$ is strongly stacked.
\end{proof}

In particular, since every strongly stacked frame is stacked, we also
obtain that \(A_j\) is stacked.

We next show that the KC property is also inherited by quotients.

\begin{prop}\label{KCquout}
Let $A$ be a KC frame and $j\in NA$. Then $A_j$ is KC.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $k\in N(A_j)$ be such that $(A_j)_k$ is compact. Then
\[
\nabla(k)\in A_j^\wedge.
\]
By Proposition \ref{fF},
\[
j_*(\nabla(k))\in A^\wedge.
\]
Consider the nucleus
\[
l=j_*\circ k\circ j^*\in NA.
\]
Since
\[
\nabla(l)=j_*(\nabla(k)),
\]
the quotient $A_l$ is compact. Since $A$ is KC, there exists $a\in A$ such that
\[
l=u_a.
\]

We claim that
\[
k=u_{j^*(a)}.
\]
Indeed, from $l=u_a$ we have
\[
a\vee x
=
l(x)
=
j_*\bigl(k(j^*(x))\bigr)
\]
for every $x\in A$. Applying $j^*$ to both sides yields
\[
j^*(a)\vee j^*(x)
=
k(j^*(x)),
\]
since $j^*\circ j_*=\id_{A_j}$.

Now let $x\in A_j$. Since $j^*\colon A\to A_j$ is surjective, we have
$j^*(x)=x$, and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_{j^*(a)}(x)
&=j^*(a)\vee x\\
&=j^*(a)\vee j^*(x)\\
&=k(j^*(x))\\
&=k(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
k=u_{j^*(a)}.
\]
Thus $(A_j)_k$ is closed. Therefore, $A_j$ is KC.
\end{proof}

We conclude this section by examining more closely the relation between
the compact quotients of a frame and those arising from its quotients.
Besides the inheritance results established above, the comparison
obtained in Porollary \ref{finftyf} induces a natural frame morphism between the
corresponding compact quotients. This construction will also play an
important role in the next section.

By Proposition \ref{finftyf}, $j\circ v_F\leq v_G\circ j$. We now compare the compact quotients induced
by \(F\) and \(G\). Consider
\[
    A_F=\{x\in A\mid v_F(x)=x\}
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    A_{j,G}=\{y\in A_j\mid v_G(y)=y\}.
\]
Define
\[
    H\colon A\longrightarrow A_{j,G},
    \qquad
    H=v_G\circ j.
\]
Since \(v_F\) is inflationary,
\[
    v_G\circ j
    \leq
    v_G\circ j\circ v_F.
\]
On the other hand, by Proposition \ref{finftyf} and the idempotency of \(v_G\),
\[
    v_G\circ j\circ v_F
    \leq
    v_G\circ v_G\circ j
    =
    v_G\circ j.
\]
Hence
\[
    H\circ v_F=H.
\]
Therefore, \(H\) factors through the quotient \(A_F\), inducing a map
\[
    h\colon A_F\longrightarrow A_{j,G}
\]
such that
\[
    h\circ v_F=v_G\circ j.
\]
Explicitly,
\[
    h(x)=v_G(j(x)),
    \qquad x\in A_F.
\]

\begin{lem}\label{bigvee g}
The map
\[
    h\colon A_F\longrightarrow A_{j,G}
\]
preserves arbitrary joins.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(X\subseteq A_F\). Since joins in \(A_F\) are given by
\[
    \bigvee_{A_F}X
    =
    v_F\left(\bigvee_A X\right),
\]
we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
    h\left(\bigvee_{A_F}X\right)
    &=
    v_G\left(
        j\left(
            v_F\left(\bigvee_A X\right)
        \right)
    \right)\\
    &\leq
    v_G\left(
        v_G\left(
            j\left(\bigvee_A X\right)
        \right)
    \right)\\
    &=
    v_G\left(
        j\left(\bigvee_A X\right)
    \right),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the inequality follows from Proposition \ref{finftyf} and the equality from
the idempotency of \(v_G\).

Since
\[
    H=v_G\circ j\colon A\longrightarrow A_{j,G}
\]
is a frame morphism,
\[
    v_G\left(j\left(\bigvee_A X\right)\right)
    =
    \bigvee_{A_{j,G}}\{H(x)\mid x\in X\}
    =
    \bigvee_{A_{j,G}}h[X].
\]
Therefore,
\[
    h\left(\bigvee_{A_F}X\right)
    \leq
    \bigvee_{A_{j,G}}h[X].
\]

Conversely, for every \(x\in X\),
\[
    x\leq\bigvee_{A_F}X,
\]
and hence, by monotonicity of \(h\),
\[
    h(x)\leq
    h\left(\bigvee_{A_F}X\right).
\]
Since this holds for every \(x\in X\),
\[
    \bigvee_{A_{j,G}}h[X]
    \leq
    h\left(\bigvee_{A_F}X\right).
\]
Thus,
\[
    h\left(\bigvee_{A_F}X\right)
    =
    \bigvee_{A_{j,G}}h[X].
\]
Therefore, \(h\) preserves arbitrary joins.
\end{proof}

The previous construction yields the following commutative diagram
induced by the compact quotients associated with the fitted nuclei.

\begin{prop}\label{VFsquare}
The diagram
\begin{equation}\label{Cuadrado}
\begin{tikzcd}
    A \arrow[r,"v_F"] \arrow[d,"j"'] &
    A_F \arrow[d,"h"] \\
    A_j \arrow[r,"v_G"'] &
    A_{j,G}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
is commutative.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma \ref{bigvee g}, \(h\) is a frame morphism. Moreover, by the definition
of \(h\),
\[
    h\circ v_F=v_G\circ j.
\]
Hence the diagram commutes.
\end{proof}

\section{The Spatial Case}\label{Analisis espacial}
We now specialize the construction obtained at the end of the previous
section to the spatial reflection. Let \(A\) be a frame and set $S=\pt A$.
For \(F\in A^\wedge\), let \(Q\in\mathcal{Q}S\) be the compact
saturated subset associated with \(F\) by the Hofmann--Mislove
correspondence, and put $G=\nabla(Q)\in(\mathcal{O}S)^\wedge$.

Taking $j=\mathcal{U}_A\colon A\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}S$ in diagram \eqref{Cuadrado}, 
we obtain the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
    A \arrow[r,"v_F"] \arrow[d,"\mathcal{U}_A"'] &
    A_F \arrow[d,"h"]\\
    \mathcal{O}S \arrow[r,"v_G"'] &
    (\mathcal{O}S)_G .
\end{tikzcd}
\]

By \cite[Lemma 5.7.1]{sexton2003}, the point space of the compact
quotient \(A_F\) is precisely \(Q\); that is,
\[
    \pt(A_F)=Q.
\]
Hence, the spatial reflection of \(A_F\) is
\[
    \mathcal{U}_{A_F}\colon
    A_F\longrightarrow
    \mathcal{O}\pt(A_F)=\mathcal{O}Q.
\]

On the other hand, since \(G=\nabla(Q)\), the points of the compact
quotient \((\mathcal{O}S)_G\) are precisely the points of \(Q\).
Thus,
\[
    \pt((\mathcal{O}S)_G)=Q,
\]
and its spatial reflection is
\[
    \mathcal{U}_{(\mathcal{O}S)_G}\colon
    (\mathcal{O}S)_G\longrightarrow
    \mathcal{O}Q.
\]

Consequently, the preceding constructions fit into the following
commutative diagram:

\begin{equation}\label{Qcuadrado}
\begin{tikzcd}
	A && {A_F} & \\
	&&& {\mathcal{O}Q} \\
	{\mathcal{O}S} && {\mathcal{O}S_\nabla}
	\arrow["{v_F}", from=1-1, to=1-3]
	\arrow["{\mathcal{U}_A}"', from=1-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["{\mathcal{U}_{A_F}}", from=1-3, to=2-4]
	\arrow["h"', from=1-3, to=3-3]
	\arrow["{v_\nabla}"', from=3-1, to=3-3]
	\arrow["{\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{O}S_\nabla}}"', from=3-3, to=2-4]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}

Since this diagram is determined by \(F\in A^\wedge\) and its
associated compact saturated subset \(Q\in\mathcal{Q}S\), we shall
refer to it as the \(Q\)-square.

Although property \(\mathbf{(H)}\) does not in general guarantee that compact
quotients are closed, the particular compact quotient appearing in the
\eqref{Qcuadrado} exhibits a closely related behavior.

\begin{thm}\label{haus1}
Under the hypotheses of the $Q$-square, if $A$ satisfies property $\mathbf{(H)}$, then
\[
\mathcal{O}Q\cong \uparrow Q'.
\]
Equivalently, the frame of open sets of the point space of $A_F$ is isomorphic to a compact closed quotient of 
a Hausdorff space.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $A$ satisfies $\mathbf{(H)}$. Since $\mathbf{(H)}$ is conservative, $S=\pt A$ is a $T_2$ space. Hence, for every $Q\in \mathcal{Q}S$, the set $Q$ is closed, and therefore $Q'\in \mathcal{O}S$.

Moreover, since every $T_2$ space is strongly stacked, we have
\[
v_\nabla=[Q'].
\]
As $Q'\in \mathcal{O}S$, it follows that 
\[
[Q']=u_{Q'}
\]

Consequently,
\[
(\mathcal{O}S)_{v_\nabla}=(\mathcal{O}S)_{u_{Q'}}\cong \uparrow Q'.
\]

Now, $u_{Q'}$ is a complemented nucleus in $\mathcal{O}S$, by \cite[Proposition VI.3.3]{PicadoPultr}, implies that $\mathcal{O}S_{v_\nabla}$ is spatial. Therefore, 
\[
(\mathcal{O}S)_{v_\nabla}\cong\mathcal{O}\pt((\mathcal{O}S)_{v_\nabla}).
\]
Since
\[
\pt((\mathcal{O}S)_{v_\nabla})=Q,
\]
we obtain
\[
\mathcal{O}Q\cong (\mathcal{O}S)_{v_\nabla}\cong \uparrow Q'.
\]
\end{proof}

We now turn to the conservativity of stackedness and strong
stackedness. For this purpose, we first establish the relation between
the transfinite iterations defining the fitted nuclei on a space and
on its frame of open sets.

\begin{lem}\label{inducciontransfinita}
Let $\alpha\in \Ord$ and $X\in CS$. Then
\[
\partial_F^\alpha(X)=\big(f_F^\alpha(X')\big)'.
\]
In particular, for every \(U\in\mathcal{O}S\),
\[
    \bigl(\partial_F^\infty(U')\bigr)'=v_F(U).
\]
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
We proceed by transfinite induction on \(\alpha\).

For \(\alpha=0\), both \(\partial_F^0\) and \(f_F^0\) are the identity. Hence,

\[
\partial_F^0(X)
=X
=(X')'
=\left(f_F^0(X')\right)'.
\]

Suppose that the result holds for some ordinal \(\alpha\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_F^{\alpha+1}(X)
&=\partial_F\left(\partial_F^\alpha(X)\right)\\
&=\left(f_F\left(\left(\partial_F^\alpha(X)\right)'\right)\right)'\\
&=\left(f_F\left(f_F^\alpha(X')\right)\right)'\\
&=\left(f_F^{\alpha+1}(X')\right)'.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally, let \(\lambda\) be a limit ordinal and suppose that the result holds for every \(\alpha<\lambda\). By definition and De Morgan's law,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\partial_F^\lambda(X)\right)'
&=
\left(\bigcap_{\alpha<\lambda}\partial_F^\alpha(X)\right)'\\
&=
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}
\left(\partial_F^\alpha(X)\right)'\\
&=
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}
f_F^\alpha(X')\\
&=
f_F^\lambda(X'),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the third equality follows from the induction hypothesis. Therefore,

\[
\partial_F^\lambda(X)
=
\left(f_F^\lambda(X')\right)'.
\]

This completes the proof.
\end{proof}


We are now in a position to prove the conservativity of both notions.
For every topological space \(S\), we have
\[
S \text{ is stacked}
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal{O}S \text{ is stacked},
\]
and
\[
S \text{ is strongly stacked}
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal{O}S \text{ is strongly stacked}.
\]

\begin{prop}\label{apiladoconservativa}
The notions of stackedness and strong stackedness are conservative.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $S\in\mathbf{Top}$ and set $A=\mathcal{O}S$.

First, suppose that $S$ is strongly stacked. Then, for every HM pair
$(F,Q)$,
\[
v_F=[Q'].
\]
By Lemma \ref{lem:auxiliar},
\[
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}=[Q'].
\]
Therefore,
\[
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}=v_F,
\]
and hence $\mathcal{O}S$ is strongly stacked.

Now, suppose that $S$ is stacked. Then, for every HM pair $(F,Q)$,
\[
Q=\partial_F^\infty(S).
\]
Hence,
\[
Q'=\left(\partial_F^\infty(S)\right)'.
\]
By Lemma \ref{inducciontransfinita},
\[
v_F(\varnothing)
=\left(\partial_F^\infty(S)\right)'
=Q'.
\]
In particular, $Q'\in \mathcal{O}S$. By Lemma \ref{lem:auxiliar},
\[
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}=[Q'].
\]
Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U})(\varnothing)
&=[Q'](\varnothing)\\
&=(Q')^\circ\\
&=Q'\\
&=v_F(\varnothing).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, $\mathcal{O}S$ is stacked.

Since all the implications used above are equivalences, the converse statements follow by reversing the argument.
\end{proof}

Recall that $A^\wedge$ is endowed with the topology generated by the basic
open sets
\[
D(x)=\{F\in A^\wedge\mid x\in F\},
\qquad x\in A.
\]
Moreover, let $VA$ denote the Vietoris modification of $A$ introduced in
\cite{simmons2004vietoris}. The points of $VA$ are of the form
\[

\qquad

a\in[v_F(0),w_F(0)].

Consider the continuous surjection
\[
\pi\colon\pt(VA)\longrightarrow A^\wedge,
\qquad
(F,a)\longmapsto F.
\]
It induces the frame morphism
\[
\mathcal{O}(\pi)=\pi^{-1}\colon
\mathcal{O}(A^\wedge)\longrightarrow
\mathcal{O}(\pt(VA)).

Recall also that the subbasic open sets of $\pt(VA)$ are determined by

(F,a)\in\Box x

\qquad
(F,a)\in\Diamond x

Particularly,
\begin{equation}\label{fibra2}
a\in [F](x)\iff x\in F\quad \text{and}\quad a\in \langle F\rangle(x)\iff x\nleq a.
\end{equation}

\begin{thm}\label{IsoVA}
Let $A$ be a frame, and consider the continuous surjection
\[
\pi\colon\pt(VA)\longrightarrow A^\wedge,
\qquad
(F,a)\longmapsto F.
\]
Then the following statements hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $A$ is $T_1$ and stacked, then
    \[
    \mathcal{O}(\pi)=\pi^{-1}\colon
    \mathcal{O}(A^\wedge)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\pt(VA))
    \]
    is an isomorphism.

    \item If $\mathcal{O}(\pi)$ is an isomorphism, then $A$ is stacked.

    \item If $\mathcal{O}(\pi)$ is an isomorphism and
    \[
    \mathcal{V}(a)=\{p\in\pt A\mid a\leq p\}\neq\varnothing
    \]
    for every $a<1$, then $A$ is $T_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
	\mbox{}
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Suppose that $A$ is $T_1$ and stacked. By Proposition \ref{focales},
\[
v_F(0)=w_F(0)
\]
for every $F\in A^\wedge$. Hence, each fiber of
\[
\pi\colon\pt(VA)\longrightarrow A^\wedge
\]
contains a unique element. Therefore, $\pi$ is bijective.

It remains to prove that
\[
\pi^{-1}\colon A^\wedge\longrightarrow\pt(VA)
\]
is continuous. Recall that the subbasic open sets of $\pt(VA)$ are given by
\[
(F,a)\in[-](x)\Longleftrightarrow x\in F
\]
and
\[
(F,a)\in\langle-\rangle(x)\Longleftrightarrow x\nleq a.
\]

For the first family,
\[
(F,a)\in[-](x)
\Longleftrightarrow
x\in F
\Longleftrightarrow
F\in D(x),
\]
and therefore
\[
\pi([-](x))=D(x).
\]

Now consider $\langle-\rangle(x)$. Since the fibers of $\pi$ collapse, for every
$(F,a)\in\pt(VA)$ we have
\[
a=v_F(0).
\]
Thus,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(F,a)\notin\langle-\rangle(x)
&\Longleftrightarrow x\leq a\\
&\Longleftrightarrow x\leq v_F(0)\\
&\Longleftrightarrow v_F(x)=v_F(0)\\
&\Longleftrightarrow x\notin F.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence,
\[
(F,a)\in\langle-\rangle(x)
\Longleftrightarrow
x\in F
\Longleftrightarrow
F\in D(x),
\]
and therefore
\[
\pi(\langle-\rangle(x))=D(x).
\]

Thus, the image under $\pi$ of every subbasic open set of $\pt(VA)$ is open in
$A^\wedge$. Hence $\pi^{-1}$ is continuous, and consequently $\pi$ is a
homeomorphism. Therefore,
\[
\mathcal{O}(\pi)=\pi^{-1}\colon
\mathcal{O}(A^\wedge)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\pt(VA))
\]
is an isomorphism.

\item Suppose that
\[
\mathcal{O}(\pi)=\pi^{-1}\colon
\mathcal{O}(A^\wedge)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\pt(VA))
\]
is an isomorphism. Since $\pi^{-1}$ is bijective, $\pi$ is injective. For every
$F\in A^\wedge$,
\[
(F,v_F(0)),(F,w_F(0))\in\pt(VA)
\]
and
\[
\pi(F,v_F(0))=F=\pi(F,w_F(0)).
\]
Hence,
\[
v_F(0)=w_F(0).
\]
By Proposition \ref{focales}, $A$ is stacked.

\item Suppose that $\mathcal{O}(\pi)$ is an isomorphism and that
\[
\mathcal{V}(a)=\{q\in\pt A\mid a\leq q\}\neq\varnothing
\]
for every $a<1$. By part~(2),
\[
v_F(0)=w_F(0)
\]
for every $F\in A^\wedge$. Therefore, by Proposition \ref{maximosenpt}, every point of $A$ is
maximal.

Let $p\in\pt A$ and $a\in A$ be such that
\[
p\leq a.
\]
By hypothesis, there exists $q\in\pt A$ such that
\[
a\leq q.
\]
Thus,
\[
p\leq a\leq q.
\]
Since $p$ and $q$ are maximal, we have $p=q$, and therefore
\[
p\leq a\leq p.
\]
Hence $a=p$, and $A$ is $T_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

We begin by recalling a relation established by Sexton in the spatial
setting. By \cite[Theorem 6.2 (a)]{sexton2006ordinal}, for every topological
space \(S\),
\[
    \mathcal{O}S\text{ neat}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    S\text{ strongly stacked}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    S\text{ stacked}.
\]
The first of these implications provides a natural starting point for
comparing neatness and strong stackedness in the point-free setting.
Indeed, although neatness of a frame does not directly imply strong
stackedness of the frame itself, it does imply strong stackedness of
its point space.

\begin{prop}
If $A$ is a neat frame, then $\pt A$ is strongly stacked.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let \(S=\pt A\). Since neatness is hereditary, \(\mathcal{O}S\) is
neat. By \cite[Theorem 6.2 (a)]{sexton2006ordinal}, \(S\) is strongly
stacked.
\end{proof}

The preceding proposition concerns the point space of \(A\), rather
than the frame \(A\) itself. It is therefore natural to ask under what
additional conditions neatness of \(A\) implies that \(A\) is strongly
stacked. The comparison induced by the spatial reflection provides
such a condition. For every HM triple $(F,Q,G)$, we have
\[
\mathcal{U}\circ v_F\leq v_G\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Suppose now that $A$ is strongly stacked. Then
\[
v_F=\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Since
\[
v_G\leq[Q'],
\]
we obtain
\[
\mathcal{U}_*\circ v_G\circ\mathcal{U}
\leq
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}
=v_F.
\]
Together with the previous inequality, this yields
\[
v_F=\mathcal{U}_*\circ v_G\circ\mathcal{U},
\]
or equivalently,
\[
\mathcal{U}\circ v_F
=
v_G\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]

Thus, strong stackedness forces the comparison induced by the spatial
reflection to commute. Under neatness, the converse also holds.
\begin{prop}\label{EficienteFapilado}
Let $A$ be a neat frame. If
\[
\mathcal{U}\circ v_F
=
v_\nabla\circ\mathcal{U}
\]
for every HM triple $(F,Q,\nabla)$, then $A$ is strongly stacked.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $S=\pt A$ and let $(F,Q,\nabla)$ be an HM triple.

Since $A$ is neat, $\mathcal{O}S$ is neat. Hence $S$ is stacked and packed.
In particular, every compact saturated subset $Q\subseteq S$ is closed, so
\[
Q'\in\mathcal{O}S.
\]

Since $S$ is stacked,
\[
Q=\partial_\nabla^\infty(S),
\]
and therefore, by Lemma \ref{inducciontransfinita},
\[
v_\nabla(\varnothing)=Q'.
\]
Because $\mathcal{O}S$ is neat, it follows that
\[
v_\nabla=u_{Q'}=[Q'].
\]

By hypothesis,
\[
\mathcal{U}\circ v_F
=
v_\nabla\circ\mathcal{U}
=
[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Applying the right adjoint $\mathcal{U}_*$, we obtain
\[
v_F
=
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Therefore, $A$ is strongly stacked.
\end{proof}

We next turn to the KC property and compare it with the preceding
notions. We first observe that KC always implies neatness.

\begin{prop}
Let $A$ be a KC-frame. Then $A$ is neat.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $F\in A^\wedge$ and put
\[
d=v_F(0).
\]
Since $A$ is KC and
\[
v_F\in[v_F,w_F],
\]
there exists $a\in A$ such that
\[
v_F=u_a.
\]
Evaluating at $0$, we obtain
\[
d=v_F(0)=u_a(0)=a.
\]
Hence
\[
v_F=u_d.
\]
Since this holds for every $F\in A^\wedge$, the frame $A$ is
neat.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}
Let $A$ be a KC frame. Then $A$ is stacked if and only if it is
strongly stacked.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $(F,Q)$ be an HM-pair and put
\[
d=v_F(0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a=w_F(0).
\]
Since every KC-frame is $T_1$, we have
\[
\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}=w_F.
\]
Moreover, by the previous proposition,
\[
v_F=u_d.
\]
Since $A$ is KC, $w_F$ is closed as well, and therefore
\[
w_F=u_a.
\]

Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
A\text{ is strongly stacked}
&\iff v_F=w_F\\
&\iff u_d=u_a\\
&\iff d=a\\
&\iff v_F(0)=w_F(0)\\
&\iff A\text{ is stacked}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since this holds for every HM-pair $(F,Q)$, the result follows.
\end{proof}

for some topological space $S$. By the spatial characterization of
neatnees, $S$ is packed and stacked. Moreover, neatness implies
that $S$ is strongly stacked. Hence $S$ is packed and strongly
stacked, and therefore $\mathcal{O}(S)$ is KC. Thus $A$ is KC.
\end{proof}

The preceding results show that KC imposes strong restrictions on the
relation between neatness and the two forms of stackedness. We now
consider converses under additional separation hypotheses.

\begin{prop}\label{KCsfitSS}
Let $A$ be a KC frame. If $A$ is subfit, then $A$ is strongly
stacked.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $(F,Q)$ be an HM-pair and put
\[
d=v_F(0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a=w_F(0).
\]
Since $A$ is KC, both $v_F$ and $w_F$ are closed nuclei. Hence
\[
v_F=u_d
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
w_F=u_a.
\]
By subfitness and \cite[Theorem~3.5]{simmons2006regularity}, we have
\[
d=a.
\]
Therefore,
\[
v_F=u_d=u_a=w_F.
\]
Since $A$ is $T_1$, we also have
\[
w_F=\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
v_F=\mathcal{U}_*\circ[Q']\circ\mathcal{U}.
\]
Since this holds for every HM pair $(F,Q)$, $A$ is strongly stacked.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{NeatfitKC}
Let \(A\) be a frame. If \(A\) is neat and fit, then \(A\) is KC.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let \(F\in A^\wedge\) and \(j\in NA\) be such that
\[
\nabla(j)=F.
\]
Since \(A\) is fit, by \cite[Theorem 3.4]{simmons2006regularity}, every nucleus on \(A\) is fitted. Hence \(j\) is the
least nucleus in its block. On the other hand, \(v_F\) is also the least
nucleus admitting \(F\). Therefore,
\[
j=v_F.
\]
Since \(A\) is neat, there exists \(d\in A\)
such that
\[
v_F=u_d.
\]
Consequently,
\[
j=u_d.
\]
Thus every nucleus whose associated filter is open is closed, and hence
\(A\) is KC.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:KC}
Every neat strongly stacked frame is KC.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $F\in A^\wedge$. Since $A$ is neat, it is $T_1$. Hence, as $A$ is
strongly stacked, we have
\[
v_F=w_F.
\]
Moreover, of neatness,
\[
v_F=u_d,
\qquad d=v_F(0).
\]
Therefore,
\[
[v_F,w_F]=\{u_d\}.
\]

Since every nucleus inducing a compact quotient of $A$ belongs to an
admissibility interval $[v_F,w_F]$ for some $F\in A^\wedge$, every such
nucleus is closed. Hence every compact quotient of $A$ is closed, and
therefore $A$ is KC.
\end{proof}

%\subsection*{The frame $[0,1]$}
Following the idea presented in \cite{simmons2006regularity} (or, alternatively, in \cite{simmons2004vietoris}), let us consider the linearly ordered frame 
\[
A=[0,1].
\] 
In this case the following observations hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For all $a,b\in A$ the implication $(a\succ b)$ is given by
\[
(a\succ b)=\begin{cases}
1, & \text{si } a\leq b,\\
b, & \text{si } a>b.
\end{cases}
\]
\item For $a, x\in A$, the distinguished nuclei $u_a, v_a, w_a$ are given by
\[
u_a(x)=\begin{cases}
  x & \text{si }a\leq x,\\
  a & \text{si }x< a,
\end{cases}, \quad v_a(x)=\begin{cases}
1 & \text{si } x\geq a,\\
a & \text{si } x<a,
\end{cases} \quad w_a(x)=\begin{cases}
1 & \text{si } x>a,\\
a & \text{si } x\leq a.
\end{cases}
\]

\item If $F\subseteq A$ is a filter, then $F$ is always admissible and has the form
\[
F=\nabla(w_a)=(a, 1]\quad \mbox{ o }\quad F=\nabla(v_a)=[a, 1]
\]
for some $a\in A$. In particular, $F\in A^\wedge$ if and only if $F=(a, 1]$.

\item If $F\in A^\wedge$, the adjusted nucleus associated $v_F$ is calculated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lm}
v_F(x)=l_m(x)=\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } x> m,\\
x & \text{if } x\leq m.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $l_m=v_m\wedge w_m$ and $m=\bigwedge F$.
\item For $S=\pt A$ it holds that $S=[0,1)$ and the open sets in $\mathcal{O}S$ are of the form
\[
\mathcal{U}_A(x)=\{p\in S\mid p<x\}=[0,x).
\]
\item The frame $A$ is not $T_1$, since no $p\in S$ is maximal. Consequently, $A$ does not satisfy any stronger separation property in $\Frm$.
\item For $F\in A^\wedge$ the compact saturated set associated $Q\in \mathcal{Q}S$ is of the form $Q=[0,m]$.
\item It holds that $[Q']=v_F$; therefore, $S$ is strongly stacked.
\item Since $[0, x)\in \mathcal{O}S$, then $[x, 1]\in \mathcal{C}S$ and $Q=[0, m]$. Therefore, the compact subsets are not closed; that is, $S$ is not packed.
\item The frame $A$ is not efficient.

\end{enumerate}

The above resume some characteristics about the frame $[0,1]$. Now, we will perform the analysis of the patch constructions of 
$A$ and its point space $S$.

Remember that for $A\in \Frm$ and $S\in \Top$ we have 
\[
\text{Pbase}=\{u_a\wedge v_F\mid a\in A, F\in A^\wedge\}\quad \text{y}\quad \text{pbase}=\{U\cap Q'\mid U\in \mathcal{O}S, Q\in \mathcal{Q}S\}
\]
where Pbase generates the patch frame of $A$ and pbase generates the topology of the patch space of $S$. By \eqref{eq:lm} we have that 
\[
\text{Pbase}=\{u_a\wedge l_m\mid a\in [0,1], m\in [0,1)\}.
\]

For $x\in A$  we have
\[
(u_a\wedge l_m)(x)=u_a(x)\wedge l_m(x).
\]

Let us analyze the value of this map according to the relative position of $x$ and $m$.
\begin{description}
\item[Case 1] If $x<m$, then 
\[
(u_a\wedge l_m)(x)=u_a(x)\wedge x=x.
\]
\item[Case 2] If $x=m$, then 
\[
(u_a\wedge l_m)(x)=u_a(m)\wedge m=x.
\]
\item[Case 3] If $x>m$, then 
\[
(u_a\wedge l_m)(x)=u_a(x)\wedge 1=u_a(x).
\]
\end{description}

In the first two cases, the value of $a$ does not affect the evaluation. Thus, the only non-trivial case occurs when $m<x$, where the value of $(u_a\wedge l_m)(x)$
depends on the relative position of $a$ and $x$. We therefore distinguish the following two subcases.
\begin{description}
\item[Case 3.1] If $m<x<a$, then 
\[
(u_a\wedge l_m)(x)=a.
\]
\item[Case 3.2] If $m<a\leq x$, then 
\[
(u_a\wedge l_m)(x)=x.
\]
\end{description}

From the preceding analysis, we conclude that the non-trivial generators of Pbase correspond precisely to the pairs $(m,a)$ with $m<a$. Consequently, 
the frame $PA$ is determined by the open intervals of the form $(m,a)$, where $m\in\pt A$ and $a\in A$.

We now turn to the question of whether $PA$ satisfies any separation property. To this end, we recall the relationship between the point-free patch construction and its spatial counterpart.

Let $A$ be an arbitrary frame and let $S=\pt A$ be its space of points. The two patch constructions are related by the following commutative diagram:
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	A & PA & NA \\
	{\mathcal{O}S} & {P\mathcal{O}S} & {N\mathcal{O}S} \\
	& {\mathcal{O}^pS} & {\mathcal{O}^fS}
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{\mathcal{U}_A}"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow[hook, from=1-2, to=1-3]
	\arrow["{P(\mathcal{U}_A)}", from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{N(\mathcal{U}_A)}", from=1-3, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=2-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow[hook, from=2-1, to=3-2]
	\arrow[hook, from=2-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow["\pi", from=2-2, to=3-2]

\end{tikzcd}\]
where $\mathcal{O}^fS$ is the Skula topology (or front topology) of $S$. In \cite{sexton2006point} is named
\emph{the patch assembly diagram} and more details about it can also be found in \cite{sexton2003point}.

In our case, $\mathcal{U}_A$ is an isomorphism. Moreover, under $\mathcal{U}_A$, the patch construction turns out to be functorial and, therefore,

To describe explicitly $P\mathcal{O}S$ and $N\mathcal{O}S$, we use the following result, which can be consulted in \cite{AvilaBezhaniMorandiZaldivar2020}.

\begin{prop}\label{Ensamble espacial}
Let $S$ be a partially ordered set.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $S$ has no infinite antichains, then $N\mathcal{O}S$ is spatial.
  \item If $S$ is totally ordered, then $N\mathcal{O}S$ is spatial.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

Thus, by Proposition \ref{Ensamble espacial} we have that $N\mathcal{O}S$ is spatial and therefore $N\mathcal{O}S\cong \mathcal{O}^fS$. With all this information,
the patch assembly diagram, in our particular case is as follows.

\[\begin{tikzcd}

	\arrow[from=2-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow[from=2-2, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
Therefore, if we want to study the frame $PA$, we can study the patch topology $\mathcal{O}^pS$. Remember that 
\[
\text{pbase}=\{U\cap Q'\mid U\in \mathcal{O}S, Q\in \mathcal{Q}S\}.
\]
In our case,
\[
\text{pbase}=\{[0, a)\cap [m, 1)\mid a\in [0,1], m\in [0,1)\}=\{(m,a)\}.
\]

Since the topology generated by the intervals $(m,a)$ coincides with the usual topology on $[0,1)$, the space $^pS$ is Hausdorff. Consequently, $\mathcal{O}^pS\cong PA$ satisfies $\mathbf{(H)}$, 
even when $A$ is not $T_1$ nor neat.
